\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
This thesis investigated the use of \citeauthor{JC:Applebaum16}'s Free XOR proof to construct a pen-and-paper \acrshort{ssp} to establish the privacy of an AND gate. Then, it attempted to encode the \acrshort{ssp} into the proof verifier, Domino, and formally validate the correctness of the encoding of the \acrshort{ssp}. 

We were able to successfully express the privacy of an AND gate as an SSP, and show that the privacy held without any specific assumptions. These games and their corresponding packages were successfully encoded in Domino, and the proof was sketched out in the form of equivalences and reductions, which were all validated by Domino, except a singular equivalence game hop. 

The work also assessed Domino as a tool for proving SSPs in the form of code. To this end, since most of the proof was successfully verified, we found the tool to be very useful. In addition, we suggested some improvements based on our usage of Domino, and hope that the proof encoding in this thesis can be useful to users of the tool. 

Finally, this work demonstrates the feasibility of constructing a \acrlong{ssp} from a different proof framework. It also demonstrates that Domino is a suitable tool for the verification of \acrlong{ssp}s.

\section{Future Work}
\label{sec:futurework}
The most immediate direction for future work is to complete the verification of the equivalence relation that remain unresolved in the current formalization. This may be aided by future improvements to the Domino language. 

On the subject of the state-separating proof itself, in this work, we chose not to use the \acrlong{pnp} optimization for \acrlong{gc}s, and assumed the special correctness property. Future work may try to model the privacy of garbling an AND gate with \acrlong{pnp}, or without the special correctness property. 
While it may be ambitious, follow up works to these may continue on to proving the privacy of a whole circuit as a \acrlong{ssp}.
